You will learn all three games by watching six people play them. None of them is perfect, and all of them change. They are fictional, as are the companies they work for, but their problems are real ones, and every claim the book makes through them is sourced in the text around them.

Dev#

Devika “Dev” Rao (she/her), 27. Three years as a QA engineer at Corvid Systems, an IT services firm, where she tests insurance software and quietly fixes the bugs she finds. She wants to build software, not just break it, and she is good enough to: she simply cannot get anyone outside Corvid to see it. Dev is careful, funny when she relaxes, and allergic to anything that feels fake. In Part I she learns the snapshot game without turning into someone she would not like. In Part II she starts a new job at Lanternfish and learns that being herself at work is a skill too. In Part III she finds out how good work gets judged: a first review, a promotion case, a fork between expert and manager, and, eventually, her next move, made from strength.

Yes, her name is Dev and she wants to be a dev. She has heard the joke.

Bea#

Beatriz “Bea” Santos (she/her), 31. The best engineer at Corvid, and possibly the best engineer Dev has ever met. Bea solves in an afternoon what takes others a week, and then mentions it to nobody. Her résumé undersells her, she freezes in phone screens, and she believes, with some evidence, that the hiring game is rigged against people like her. Bea is all film and no trailer.

Rex#

Rex Calloway (he/him), 29. Dev’s desk neighbour at Corvid, for now. Rex interviews brilliantly: warm, quick, quotable. He has had six jobs in eight years and has left each a little earlier than the one before. He is not a villain; he is generous, good company and better at the snapshot game than anyone in this book. He is all trailer. Part II asks him to show the film, and Part III gives him the promotion he always wanted, which turns out to be his hardest lesson.

Meera#

Meera Iyer (she/her), 54. Dev’s first team lead, now an engineering director elsewhere. Meera has hired more than two hundred people and fired a few, and she will tell you the second taught her more. She plays the voice from the other side of the table: what hiring looks like to the people doing it. Her advice is good, but it is advice, and the book marks where her experience ends and the evidence begins. In Part III, thirty years into her own career, she is the book’s view of a working life over decades.

Tomás#

Tomás Rivera (he/him), 41. The engineering manager at Lanternfish who interviews Dev in Part I, becomes her manager in Part II, and writes her reviews in Part III, which means he has to live with what he hired. He is calm, direct and allergic to surprises, especially the kind that turn up on a Friday afternoon.

Kai#

Kai Mensah (they/them), 26. A product designer at Lanternfish and Dev’s first friend there. Kai is open, quick to laugh, and quick to share things Dev would keep to herself. Through Kai, Part II looks at office friendship: its warmth, its pull and its boundaries. In Part III, Kai takes the manager’s path that Dev is unsure about.

Running jokes#

A few things come back. You do not need to track them, but you will be rewarded if you do.

  • The Spreadsheet. Dev runs her job search from a spreadsheet that grows tabs the way a houseplant grows leaves. Watch the tab names.
  • Meera’s mugs. Meera never appears without a mug, and the slogan on it is never an accident.
  • Rex’s headline. Rex’s professional profile headline gets grander every time he changes jobs.
  • Old Faithful. The coffee machine at Lanternfish, which is neither old nor faithful. How people respond when it breaks tells you a lot about them.
