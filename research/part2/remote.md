# Remote and hybrid work; proximity bias

Research notes for Part II. Grades and labels as in `README.md`; keys refer to `sources.yaml`.

The evidence here is unusually strong: randomised trials and large natural experiments. It also shows real trade-offs, and the book should resist both "remote is always better" and "the office is essential".

---

## 1. Performance and retention

**Evidence**

- Randomised experiment at a Chinese travel agency's call centre: working from home for nine months raised performance 13% and halved attrition. But **promotion rates conditional on performance fell**, and over half the volunteers later chose to return to the office. **[strong]** `bloom2015`
- RCT with 1,612 employees at a Chinese tech company (2021-22): two WFH days a week cut quit rates by a third, with no measurable effect on performance grades or promotions over two years. Managers' views of hybrid improved after the trial. **[strong]** `bloom2024`
- Natural experiment at the US Patent and Trademark Office: moving from work-from-home to work-from-anywhere raised output 4.4% without more rework. **[moderate]** `choudhury2021`
- Pandemic WFH at an Indian IT firm (10,000+ professionals): hours rose, productivity fell 8-19%, with more time spent coordinating and fewer one-to-ones with supervisors. **[moderate]** `gibbs2023` (forced WFH)
- Meta-analysis of pre-2007 telecommuting (46 studies): small, mostly beneficial effects on autonomy, work-family conflict, satisfaction and performance; more than 2.5 days a week harmed coworker relationships. **[strong]** `gajendran2007`

## 2. Collaboration, learning and juniors

- Firm-wide remote work at Microsoft (61,182 employees) made collaboration networks more static and siloed, with fewer bridging ties and more asynchronous communication. **[strong]** `yang2022`
- Sitting near teammates increased coding feedback by 18.3% and improved code quality, with gains concentrated among junior engineers; senior engineers produced less code. In national data, young graduates in remotable jobs did relatively worse after the pandemic. **[strong]** `emanuel2026` (published version; the earlier working paper `emanuel2023` reported 22% more feedback)

**Researcher's synthesis**: proximity is a *mentoring subsidy* paid by seniors for juniors. That makes it a career-stage question: early-career readers have the most to gain from in-person time, while experienced people gain focus time from remote work.

## 3. Proximity bias and "face time"

- Simply being *seen* at work leads observers to spontaneously infer traits: dependable for normal hours, committed for extra hours. **[moderate]** `elsbach2010`
- Distributed employees compensate with commitment signals that win better work, but these escalate into felt sacrifice that headquarters doesn't notice. **[moderate]** `cristea2019`
- In the 2015 experiment, home workers' promotion rate, conditional on performance, fell. **[strong]** `bloom2015`. The 2024 hybrid trial found **no** promotion difference. **[strong]** `bloom2024`

**Synthesis**: proximity bias is plausible and partly evidenced; whether it hurts promotions seems to depend on the setup (fully remote vs hybrid).

## 4. Loneliness and open offices

- Fully remote employees reported more daily loneliness (25%) than hybrid (21%) or on-site (16%) workers. **[moderate]** `gallup2024`
- Open-plan offices *reduced* face-to-face interaction by about 70% at two headquarters. **[moderate]** `bernstein2018`. Being physically present doesn't guarantee interaction.
