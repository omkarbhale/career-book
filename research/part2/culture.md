# Cultural variation: Hofstede, Meyer's Culture Map, tightness

Research notes for Part II. Grades and labels as in `README.md`; keys refer to `sources.yaml`.

---

## 1. Hofstede's dimensions

**Evidence**

- Hofstede's model has six dimensions of national culture: power distance, uncertainty avoidance, individualism/collectivism, masculinity/femininity, long/short-term orientation, and indulgence/restraint. Hofstede himself warns against applying them to individuals. **[moderate]** `hofstede2011` (by the model's author)
- Meta-analysis (598 studies, 200,000+ people): the original four dimensions predict work outcomes with a modest average effect (ρ = 0.18), more for emotions and attitudes than for job performance, and more strongly in "tight" cultures. **[strong]** `taras2010`
- **About 80% of the variation in cultural values lies *within* countries, not between them.** Country is a poor proxy for an individual's culture. **[strong]** `taras2016`
- Critique of the model's assumptions (e.g. treating one company's employees as standing for a nation). **[moderate]** `mcsweeney2002`

## 2. Tight vs loose cultures

- Across 33 nations, cultures differ in "tightness": how strong norms are and how much deviance is tolerated. This links to ecological and historical threats. **[strong]** `gelfand2011`

## 3. Erin Meyer's Culture Map (practitioner framework)

- Eight scales: communicating (low vs high context), evaluating (direct vs indirect negative feedback), persuading, leading, deciding, trusting, disagreeing, scheduling. **[weak]** as evidence. `meyer2014`
- It is useful vocabulary, especially "evaluating", which explains why one culture's polite feedback sounds harsh, or vague, to another. But country placements are generalisations, and `taras2016` warns that most variation is within countries. The author also sells tools and workshops.

**Researcher's synthesis**: use culture frameworks as *hypotheses* about a colleague, never as conclusions. Ask how the person likes feedback; don't assume from a passport. Organisational and team culture can matter as much as national culture (author's view).
