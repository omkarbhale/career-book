## 1. What burnout is (and isn't)

**Evidence**

- WHO's ICD-11 lists burn-out as an **occupational phenomenon, not a medical condition**: a syndrome from chronic workplace stress that has not been successfully managed, with three dimensions (exhaustion; mental distance, cynicism or negativity toward the job; reduced professional efficacy). It applies only to work. **[strong]** `who2019`
- Maslach's model has the same three dimensions and places burnout in the *relationship* between people and their work, not only in the individual. **[strong]** `maslach2001`, `maslach2016`. Flag: the Maslach Burnout Inventory is a commercially sold instrument.
- Measurement is a mess: physician burnout prevalence estimates ranged from 0% to 80.5% across 182 studies using at least 142 different definitions. **[strong]** `rotenstein2018`. **Lesson for the book**: be very sceptical of headline "X% of workers are burned out" statistics.

## 2. Causes

- Job Demands-Resources model: strain comes from high demands; motivation comes from resources such as support, autonomy and feedback, which buffer demands. **[strong]** `bakker2007` (widely used; critics say too flexible)
- Surface acting (faking emotions) relates substantially to impaired well-being. **[strong]** `hulsheger2011` (see `authenticity.md` §3)
- Helping beyond one's role can lead to overload and work-family conflict. **[moderate]** `bolino2005`
- Long hours: working 55+ hours a week was linked to an estimated 745,000 deaths from heart disease and stroke in 2016 (WHO/ILO). **[strong]** `pega2021` (population estimates)
- Workplace stressors (long hours, insecurity, low control, work-family conflict and others) were estimated to be associated with over 120,000 US deaths a year. **[moderate]** `goh2016` (model with wide uncertainty)
- Expectations to monitor email after hours raise anxiety and harm health, for partners too, regardless of actual time spent on email. **[moderate]** `becker2021`, `belkin2020`
- Interruptions: people compensate by working faster, at the cost of more stress and frustration. **[moderate]** `mark2008`. The popular "23 minutes to refocus" figure is **not** from this paper; see `myths.md`.

## 3. What helps

- Mentally detaching from work in off-hours relates to better well-being without lower engagement. **[strong]** `sonnentag2012`
- Burnout interventions for physicians had *small* effects (SMD -0.29); **organisation-directed** interventions worked better (-0.45) than individual-directed ones (-0.18). **[strong]** `panagioti2017`
- A sender's note clarifying that a reply isn't urgent reduces receivers' stress. **[strong]** `giurge2021`
- "Willpower is a tank that runs dry" (ego depletion) is **not** well supported; a 23-lab replication found a near-zero effect. **[strong]** `hagger2016`. See `myths.md`.

**Researcher's synthesis**: burnout is mostly about the job, not about personal weakness. The individual levers (detachment, boundaries, saying no, choosing deep over surface acting) help at the margin; the big levers are workload, control and support, which is why changing teams or jobs is sometimes the right answer (a bridge back to Part I).
