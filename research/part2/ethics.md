## 1. How good people do bad things

**Evidence**

- Meta-analysis: unethical choices come from individual traits ("bad apples"), the moral issue itself ("bad cases") and the environment ("bad barrels") together. **[strong]** `kishgephart2010`
- Small transgressions that escalate gradually lead to larger ones (a "slippery slope"), through moral disengagement. **[moderate]** `welsh2015`
- "Ethical fading": self-deception lets the ethical side of a decision fade from view. **[moderate]** theory, title-level. `tenbrunsel2004`
- Goals that are too specific or too hard can narrow focus and increase unethical behaviour, among other side effects. **[moderate]** `ordonez2009` (a contested critique of goal setting)

**Researcher's synthesis**: pressure rarely announces itself as an ethics test. It shows up as a target, a deadline or a small favour, so the useful skill is noticing the first small step. Set your lines in advance, in calm conditions.

## 2. Speaking up

- Fraud in large US companies was mostly detected by employees, media and industry regulators, not auditors or the SEC. **[strong]** `dyck2010`
- A meta-analysis examines the correlates of whistleblowing intentions, actual whistleblowing and retaliation. **[moderate]** `mesmermagnus2005` (title-level only)
- Challenging voice can be penalised by managers. **[moderate]** `burris2012`; psychological safety is linked to voice. **[strong]** `edmondson2014`, `frazier2017`

## 3. A cautionary tale inside the evidence base itself

- "Signing at the top of a form reduces cheating" (Shu et al. 2012) is **retracted** (Crossref and OpenAlex both flag it). **[myth]** `shu2012`
- "The moral virtue of authenticity" (Gino et al. 2015) is **retracted**. **[myth]** `gino2015`
- These are useful *in* the book as an honest aside: even honesty research has had honesty problems, which is why this book grades its sources.
