## 1. Why people actually leave

**Evidence**

- Pew (Feb 2022): among US workers who quit in 2021, the top reasons were low pay (63%), no advancement opportunities (63%) and feeling disrespected at work (57%), followed by childcare (48%), inflexible hours (45%) and benefits (43%). **[strong]** `pew2022` (self-reported reasons)
- Turnover meta-analyses show many antecedents (attitudes, alternatives, embeddedness, leadership and more) with context-dependent effects. **[strong]** `rubenstein2018`, `griffeth2000`. The abstracts give no effect sizes, so check the full text before quoting numbers.
- Gallup's "one in two have left a job to get away from their manager" and "managers account for at least 70% of the variance in engagement". **[weak]** `gallup2015` (commercial; inconsistent wording on the page). See `myths.md` on "people leave managers, not companies".

## 2. How you resign matters

- Employees resign in identifiable styles, shaped by their relationship with the employer and boss, and the style affects how their managers react emotionally. **[moderate]** `klotz2016`. Check the named styles against the full text before quoting them.
- "Boomerang" managers rehired by a large organisation performed about the same as other hires in year one but improved less over time and were more likely to leave again, often for the same reasons. **[strong]** `arnold2021`
- What boomerang employees learned while away predicted their performance on return (professional basketball). **[weak]** for offices. `swider2017`

**Researcher's synthesis**: leaving is the last move in the repeated game (`first-principles.md` §1), and the game isn't over. References, boomerang chances and your reputation all travel. Leave the way you'd want to be remembered: give notice, hand over properly, and don't torch anything (author's view, consistent with `klotz2016`).

## 3. Layoffs

- Downsizing predicts higher voluntary turnover among survivors, through lower commitment; fair procedures and practices that embed employees soften it. **[strong]** `trevor2008`
- Review of the causes and consequences of downsizing. **[moderate]** `datta2010`
- Unemployment is associated with lower psychological and physical well-being; coping resources and appraisals matter more than demographics. **[strong]** `mckeeryan2005`
- For high-seniority men in mass layoffs (Pennsylvania, 1970s-80s), mortality in the year after displacement was 50-100% higher than expected, and still 10-15% higher two decades later. **[strong]** `sullivan2009` (old data, one US state, men)

**Researcher's synthesis**: being laid off is not a verdict on your character, and the evidence says it is a real health shock. Treat it as one: use social support, structure your time, and get back to Part I. Survivors should know that a layoff often triggers more voluntary exits.
