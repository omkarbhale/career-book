# Part III key findings index: growth at work

One line per key finding, in the form: finding (grade; source key; file). Grades and method are in `README.md`; claim verdicts are in `myths.md`. Keys resolve in this part's `sources.yaml` unless marked (Part I) or (Part II).

## Performance reviews and ratings
- Rater idiosyncrasy explained 62% and 53% of variance in 360-degree ratings; actual performance explained 21% and 25%. (strong; `scullen2000`; reviews.md)
- Two supervisors rating the same person agree at a mean reliability of .52. (strong; `viswesvaran1996`; reviews.md)
- Managers weight task, citizenship and counterproductive behaviour differently when forming one overall rating. (moderate; `rotundo2002`; reviews.md)
- Ratings diverge from performance through error, non-performance factors and deliberate distortion; raters need tools *and* incentives to be honest. (moderate; `murphy2008`; reviews.md)
- Performance-management failures stem from formal steps disconnected from day-to-day guidance, not from the rating form. (moderate; `pulakos2011`, `denisi2017`; reviews.md)
- In one tech firm's reviews, women were more often "too aggressive" and men "too soft"; "taking charge" earned top ratings only for men. (moderate; `correll2020`; reviews.md)
- Forced distribution could raise workforce potential in simulation, mostly in the first years. (moderate; `scullen2005`; reviews.md)
- Raters find forced distribution harder and less fair, especially for pay decisions. (moderate; `schleicher2009`; reviews.md)
- Forced ratings reduced knowledge sharing and slowed teamwork in experiments. (moderate; `loberg2021`; reviews.md)
- Forced ratings raised stress and made ratings track gaming and eloquence more than actual creativity. (moderate; `cardinaels2021`; reviews.md)
- Microsoft ended its forced curve ("No more curve") in November 2013. (moderate; `shrm2013`; reviews.md)
- Post-calibration ratings correlated better with other performance measures than pre-calibration ratings. (moderate; `speer2019`; reviews.md)
- Experts disagree on abolishing ratings; evaluation happens regardless. (moderate; `adler2016`; reviews.md)

## Self-assessment and feedback seeking
- Self-evaluations of ability correlate .29 on average with objective performance (22 meta-analyses). (strong; `zell2014`; self-assessment.md)
- Self-estimated and measured cognitive ability correlate .33. (strong; `freund2012`; self-assessment.md)
- Self-supervisor agreement is ρ ≈ .34; self-ratings are more lenient in Western samples (d = 0.32). (strong; `heidemeier2009`, `harris1988`; self-assessment.md)
- Bottom-quartile scorers estimated themselves at the 62nd percentile while scoring at the 12th (original Dunning-Kruger). (moderate; `kruger1999`; self-assessment.md)
- The Dunning-Kruger pattern is largely reproduced by regression and the better-than-average effect, and even by random numbers. (moderate; `krueger2002`, `nuhfer2016`; self-assessment.md)
- With valid methods, any Dunning-Kruger effect is small and confined to very low scorers. (moderate; `gignac2020`, `gignac2024`; self-assessment.md)
- A large replication supports a real but modest deficit in low performers' ability to judge their own answers. (strong; `jansen2021`; self-assessment.md)
- Women rate their own performance lower than equally performing men on male-typed tasks, even without incentives. (strong; `exley2022`; self-assessment.md)
- Overconfidence raises status in groups by making people look competent. (moderate; `anderson2012`; self-assessment.md)
- Feedback seeking is driven by instrumental, ego and image motives. (strong; `ashford2003`, `ashford2016`; self-assessment.md)
- Feedback seeking declines with tenure and has a small direct link to performance. (strong; `anseel2015` (Part II); self-assessment.md)

## Promotions
- Firms promote top salespeople although sales performance predicts worse managerial performance (consistent with the Peter Principle). (strong; `benson2019`; promotions.md)
- Some post-promotion decline is regression to the mean, which needs no incompetence. (moderate, theory; `lazear2004`; promotions.md)
- Women received higher performance but lower "potential" ratings; potential gaps explain about half the gender promotion gap, and women then outperformed. (strong; `benson2026`; promotions.md)
- More face time with one's manager speeds promotion; this could explain a third of one firm's gender promotion gap. (strong; `cullen2023b`; promotions.md)
- Promotions work like rank-order tournaments; pay jumps are prizes. (moderate, theory; `lazear1981`; promotions.md)
- Within one firm, promotion fast tracks exist and pay varies widely within levels. (moderate; `baker1994`; promotions.md)
- Internal promotees outperformed external hires for two years; external hires were paid ~18% more. Part I's grade is upgraded from moderate. (strong; `bidwell2011`; promotions.md)
- For every 100 men promoted to manager, 93 women were (2025); 31% of entry-level women vs 45% of men report a sponsor. (moderate, commercial and advocacy; `leanin2025`; promotions.md)
- Promotions raise job satisfaction briefly but have negligible effects on health and happiness; mental health worsens after two years. (moderate; `johnston2013`, `boyce2012`; promotions.md)
- Big-tech promotions use packets judged by committees against level definitions. (weak, practitioner; `orosz2019`; promotions.md)
- Levels frameworks describe impact at the next level and are "not a promotion checklist". (weak, practitioner; `todi2021`, `larson2020`; promotions.md)

## Getting good
- Deliberate practice explained 18% of sports performance overall but 1% among elite athletes. (strong; `macnamara2016`; getting-good.md)
- Ericsson's reanalysis: strictly defined deliberate practice explains more (29%) than loosely defined practice. (moderate; `ericsson2019`; getting-good.md)
- Amount of experience (ρ = .43) predicts performance better than time served; overall ρ = .27. (strong; `quinones1995`; getting-good.md)
- Physicians' quality of care more often declines than rises with years in practice. (strong; `choudhry2005`; getting-good.md)
- Intuition is trustworthy only in regular environments with chances to learn through feedback; confidence is not a guide. (moderate; `kahneman2009`; getting-good.md)
- Spaced practice beats cramming; optimal spacing grows with the retention interval. (strong; `cepeda2006`; getting-good.md)
- Practice testing and spacing are high-utility techniques; rereading and highlighting are low. (strong; `dunlosky2013`; getting-good.md)
- No adequate evidence supports teaching to learning styles. (strong; `pashler2008`; getting-good.md)
- Growth-mindset effects on achievement are weak overall. (strong; `sisk2018`; getting-good.md)
- The "70% rule" behind 70-20-10 rests on weak evidence. (moderate; `clardy2018`; getting-good.md)
- New leaders learn their role faster when their boss both models and explains the job. (moderate; `dragoni2014`; getting-good.md, the-fork.md)
- Career capital = skills, connections, credentials, character, runway. (weak, practitioner; `todd2026`; getting-good.md)

## The fork: IC or manager
- Engineers' preferences shift with age toward interesting work over either managerial or technical-ladder advancement. (moderate; `allen1986`; the-fork.md)
- Your supervisor's ladder steers which ladder you climb. (moderate; `katz1995`; the-fork.md)
- A boss's technical competence is the strongest predictor of worker job satisfaction. (moderate; `artz2017`; the-fork.md)
- Median US pay, May 2025: software developers $135,980; computer and information systems managers $175,140. (strong; `bls2026a`, `bls2026b`; the-fork.md)
- Higher depression and anxiety in "contradictory class locations" (US workers). (moderate; `prins2015`; the-fork.md)
- Manager engagement fell from 30% to 27% in 2024 (individual contributors 18%). (weak, commercial; `gallup2025`; the-fork.md)
- Four Staff-plus archetypes: Tech Lead, Architect, Solver, Right Hand. (weak, practitioner; `larson2020`; the-fork.md)
- "Management is not a promotion, it's a change of profession"; the pendulum model. (weak, practitioner; `majors2017`; the-fork.md)

## The long game
- Career plateaus link to lower satisfaction and performance, largely through perceived lack of support; proactivity helps. (moderate; `yang2019`, `ference1977`; long-game.md)
- Skill obsolescence flattens earnings in fast-changing careers; the applied STEM premium falls by more than half in a decade. (strong; `deming2020`; long-game.md)
- Occupational mobility in the US was high and rising, and human capital is largely occupation-specific. (strong; `kambourov2008`; long-game.md)
- The "five-year skills half-life" traces to a hedged aside with no data. (myth-or-unsupported; `brown2026`, `campbell2010`; long-game.md)
- Age is largely unrelated to core task performance. (strong; `ng2008`; long-game.md)
- A cross-sectional happiness U-curve bottoms out around age 50 in 145 countries, but this is contested. (moderate; `blanchflower2021`, `galambos2020`; long-game.md)

## Motivation and meaning
- Need satisfaction (competence, autonomy, relatedness) supports autonomous motivation and wellness. (strong; `deci2017`, `vandenbroeck2016`; motivation.md)
- Intrinsic motivation predicts performance (ρ = .21-.45) with or without incentives; it predicts quality, and incentives predict quantity. (strong; `cerasoli2014`; motivation.md)
- Job satisfaction and performance correlate about .30. (strong; `judge2001`; motivation.md)
- Job-crafting interventions raise engagement (g = 0.31) and contextual performance (g = 0.39). (moderate; `oprea2019`, `wrzesniewski2001`; motivation.md)
- Self-chosen job titles reduced emotional exhaustion. (moderate; `grant2014`; motivation.md)
- Believing passion is fixed narrows interests and makes difficulty more demotivating. (moderate; `okeefe2018`; motivation.md)
- Both "fit" and "develop" beliefs about passion can lead to success. (moderate; `chen2015`; motivation.md)
- Callings bring meaning plus sacrifice of pay and time. (moderate; `bunderson2009`; motivation.md)
- Observers see exploiting passionate workers as more legitimate. (moderate; `kim2020`; motivation.md)
- People accept lower pay for meaningful work. (moderate; `hu2017`; motivation.md, money.md)

## Money and happiness
- Emotional well-being plateaued at ~$75,000 in 2010 data. (strong, now superseded; `kahneman2010`; money.md)
- Experienced well-being rises linearly with log income with no plateau. (strong; `killingsworth2021`; money.md)
- The adversarial collaboration found flattening around $100,000 only for the least happy 15-20%. (strong; `killingsworth2023`; money.md)
- Lottery wealth raises life satisfaction for over a decade, with smaller effects on happiness. (strong; `lindqvist2020`; money.md)
- Happiness adapts to income within about four years but not to status. (moderate; `ditella2010`; money.md)
- Income losses hurt more than equivalent gains help. (strong; `boyce2013`; money.md)
- Learning you're paid below your unit's median lowers satisfaction and raises job search; pay rank beats pay level. (strong; `card2012`; money.md)
- Satisfaction falls over time in the same job even as wages rise. (moderate; `grund2007`; money.md)

## Regional and cultural
- Japan's seniority wages and lifetime employment are eroding; age-wage profiles have flattened. (strong; `hamaaki2012`; regional.md)
- Up-or-out contracts solve a two-sided commitment problem. (moderate, theory; `kahn1988`; regional.md)
- US firms cluster "manager" titles just above the overtime-exemption salary threshold. (moderate; `cohen2023`; regional.md)
- UK retail "managers" earning under 400 GBP a week rose from 37% to nearly 60% in the 2000s. (weak; `resolution2012`; regional.md)
