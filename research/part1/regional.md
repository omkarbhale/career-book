# Regional and cultural variation: US, UK/EU, India

Research notes for Part I, written as raw material for sidebars. Most rows point to the detailed topic files. Keys refer to `sources.yaml`. Rows marked *practitioner* or *unverified* must not be printed as fact without a source.

## At a glance

| Topic | US | UK / EU | India |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Document** | "Resume", usually 1–2 pages; a photo is discouraged. The EEOC says employers should not ask for a photo before an offer (`eeoc-prohibited`). | UK "CV": "You should not include your age, your date of birth, whether you're married or your nationality" (`ncs-cv`, National Careers Service). The EU's Europass CV tool tells users to "add a professional photograph of yourself" (`europass-cv`). That is consistent with photos being more customary in parts of continental Europe than in the UK or US *(the prevalence is unverified)*. | "Resume/CV"; some sectors still use "biodata" with personal details *(practitioner, unverified)*. |
| **Discrimination in screening** | Callback gaps by race are large and stable since 1989 (`quillian2017`, `kline2022`, `bertrand2004`). | Not separately verified in this pass. A British GEMM/Nuffield audit exists but was not opened. | Caste and religion signals changed interview odds: about ⅔ for Dalit and ⅓ for Muslim applicants vs high-caste Hindu names (`thorat2009`, via summary). |
| **Interview questions** | Pre-offer disability questions are explicitly prohibited; other protected-trait questions are evidence of discriminatory intent (`eeoc-prohibited`). | Health and disability questions are generally unlawful before an offer under Equality Act 2010 s.60 (`ukparl2020-health`). | No general private-sector statute was verified in this pass *(unverified)*. |
| **Pay transparency** | 13 states plus DC require ranges in postings as of May 2026; VA and ME from 2026, DE from 2027 (`clockspot-paytransparency`, `cooley2026`, `nydol-paytransparency`). | Directive (EU) 2023/970: pay information before employment and a ban on asking salary history. Transposition was due 7 June 2026, and most states were late (`ec-paytransparency`, `lockton2026-eu`). The UK is not covered (no longer in the EU). | No posting mandate verified *(unverified)*. Pay is quoted as **CTC (cost to company)**, which includes components that are not take-home pay (`tickertape-ctc`, weak). Under the new Labour Codes, a single "wages" definition with a 50% ceiling on excluded components changes the base for PF and gratuity (`argus-labourcodes`). |
| **Notice when resigning** | At-will; no legal notice required. Two weeks is the custom *(practitioner)* (`lii-atwill`). | UK: at least 1 week statutory, contract usually longer (`govuk-notice`). Germany: 4 weeks basic (`bgb622`). | By contract: 1–2 months for non-managerial roles, 3–6 months for senior ones (`khaitan-notice`). Final dues are payable within 2 days of resignation under the Labour Codes, in force 21 Nov 2025 (`argus-labourcodes`). |
| **Employer notice and layoffs** | WARN Act: 60 days' notice for covered closings and mass layoffs (`warn-2102`, `warn-2101`). | UK statutory scale up to 12 weeks (`govuk-notice`). Germany up to 7 months by service (`bgb622`). | Industrial Relations Code governs layoff, retrenchment and closure (`barandbench-labourcodes`); details not verified here. |
| **Non-competes** | Federal FTC ban vacated, appeal dismissed 8 Sep 2025; state law governs. California voids them (`uschamber-ryan`, `loeb2023-california`). | UK: enforceable if reasonable; a 3-month cap was proposed in 2023, not enacted; options reopened Nov 2025 (`cms2025-uk-noncompete`). Garden leave is common (`govuk-notice`). | Post-employment non-competes are generally void under Contract Act s.27 (`barandbench-s27`). |
| **Background checks** | FCRA: written consent; report copy before adverse action (`eeoc-ftc-background`). Ban-the-box laws can widen racial gaps (`agan2018`). | UK DBS: basic, standard, enhanced (`govuk-dbs`). | Third-party background verification (BGV) is widespread in IT *(practitioner, unverified)*. |
| **Recruitment agencies** | Employer-paid; fees of roughly 15–25% are typical *(vendor sources)* (`topechelon-fees`). | GB: agencies "cannot charge you a fee for finding or trying to find you work" (`govuk-agency-fees`). | Candidate-fee scams are a known risk *(practitioner, unverified)*. |
| **Networks** | 50–60% of US job seekers report using a referral (`lester2021`). | 25–40% in most European countries heard of their job via informal contacts (`pellizzari-via-brown2016`). | Not verified with Indian data in this pass. |
| **Tenure** | Median 4.1 years (Jan 2026); 3.0 years for ages 25–34 (`bls-tenure2026`). | Not verified. | Not verified. |
