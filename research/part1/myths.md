# Myths and pop-psychology claims about getting a job: verdicts

Each claim gets a verdict, a grade (see `README.md`) and source keys (see `sources.yaml`). The detail lives in the topic files. Verdicts: **False** (contradicted by good evidence), **Unsupported** (no traceable evidence; may or may not be true), **Overstated** (a real effect inflated), **Half-true** (true core, misleading packaging), **True** (holds up).

| # | Claim | Verdict | What the evidence says | Grade | Keys | File |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "ATS software auto-rejects 75% of resumes before a human sees them." | **Unsupported** | Secondary sources trace the figure to a 2012 sales claim by Preptel, a resume-optimisation vendor that closed in 2013; no study was ever published. What is real: employers configure knockout filters (degrees, exact credentials, gaps). 88% of employers agree qualified candidates are filtered out for not matching exact criteria. | myth-or-unsupported | `interviewguys-ats-myth`, `hiringthing-ats-myth`, `hiddenworkers2021`, `enhancv2025-ats` | hiring-funnel.md |
| 2 | "Recruiters look at your resume for 6 seconds." | **Overstated** | It comes from TheLadders' 2012 eye-tracking study of 30 recruiters, which also sold resume rewrites. The 2018 update says 7.4 s, with no sample size given. It measures the first keep/skip glance, not the evaluation. | weak | `ladders2012`, `ladders2018` | hiring-funnel.md |
| 3 | "Every corporate job gets 250 applications." | **Unsupported as a number** | It comes from a 2013 ERE article by a consultant, hedged in the original ("varies with the company and the job"), with no method; often misattributed to Glassdoor. Vendor data shows applicants per hire anywhere from dozens to hundreds. | weak | `sullivan2013`, `careerplug2025`, `gem2025` | hiring-funnel.md |
| 4 | "70–80% of jobs are never advertised (the hidden job market)." | **Half-true** | No traceable source for the percentage. But 50–60% of workers report finding jobs with a contact's help, and referrals and internal moves are heavily over-represented among hires. | myth-or-unsupported (the %), strong (the mechanism) | `fresnostate2024-hidden`, `lester2021`, `brown2016`, `gem2025` | networking.md |
| 5 | "It's not what you know, it's who you know." | **Half-true** | Referred candidates are 6% of applicants but 29% of hires in one firm, but referrals mostly work by carrying *information about fit*. Weak ties help most at moderate strength. | strong | `brown2016`, `burks2015`, `rajkumar2022` | referrals.md |
| 6 | "Weak ties are always better than strong ties." | **Overstated** | The LinkedIn experiment found an inverted U. Facebook data shows most help arrives via weak ties, but each strong tie is worth more. | strong | `rajkumar2022`, `gee2017` | networking.md |
| 7 | "Women only apply when they meet 100% of the requirements; men apply at 60%." | **Unsupported as stated** | It comes from an unpublished Hewlett-Packard internal report cited in a 2014 HBR article. A 2024 experimental test found no gender difference in Study 1 and a "not robust" one later. | myth-or-unsupported | `mohr2014`, `salwender2024` | positioning.md |
| 8 | "Cover letters are dead / always essential." | **Unsupported either way** | Only commercial evidence: a tailored letter came with 53% more callbacks in a resume company's field test; hiring-manager surveys disagree. | weak | `resumego2020-cover`, `aacsb2020-twimc` | positioning.md |
| 9 | "Never address a cover letter 'To Whom It May Concern'." | **Probably false** | In a commercial survey of 1,000+ hiring managers, 83% said it has little or no impact. | weak | `aacsb2020-twimc` | positioning.md |
| 10 | "Your resume must be one page." | **Unsupported as a rule** | A commercial simulation found two-page resumes preferred 2.3×, and 1.4× at entry level. No peer-reviewed evidence either way. | weak | `resumego2018-length` | positioning.md |
| 11 | "LinkedIn profiles with photos get 21× more views." | **Unsupported** | LinkedIn's own marketing claim, with no published method or dataset. | myth-or-unsupported | `sej-linkedin-photo` | positioning.md |
| 12 | "A typo or two doesn't matter." | **False** | Recruiter experiment: even a few errors cut interview probability by 7.3 points; many errors by 18.5. | strong | `sterkens2023` | positioning.md |
| 13 | "Good interviewers can judge people from gut feel in an unstructured chat." | **False** | Unstructured interviews have validity .19 vs .42 for structured, in the revised estimates. Google called brainteasers "a complete waste of time". | strong | `sackett2022`, `abc2013-google` | selection-methods.md |
| 14 | "Cognitive ability tests are by far the best predictor of job performance (.51)." | **Overstated / contested** | Revised to .31 (Sackett 2022) and .22 using 21st-century data (Sackett 2024). Others dispute the corrections. Structured interviews now rank first. | strong (with an active debate) | `schmidt1998`, `sackett2022`, `sackett2023-gca`, `ones2023` | selection-methods.md |
| 15 | "Interviewers make up their minds in the first 7 seconds / 90 seconds." | **Overstated** | Early impressions correlate with later ratings (r = .42) and offers (r = .22), and leak into early answers. No evidence of a fixed decision point. "Thin slices" research was on teachers, not hiring. | strong (effect), myth-or-unsupported (timing) | `barrick2010`, `swider2016`, `ambady1993` | interviewing.md |
| 16 | "93% of communication is nonverbal (Mehrabian's 7-38-55)." | **False as generalised** | Mehrabian's own site: the equations come from experiments on communicating feelings and attitudes (like/dislike). "Unless a communicator is talking about their feelings or attitudes, these equations are not applicable." | strong | `mehrabian-site`, `bigthink-mehrabian` | interviewing.md (sidebar) |
| 17 | "Power-pose for two minutes before your interview to boost testosterone and confidence." | **False (hormones and behaviour), weak (feelings)** | The first author no longer believes the effects are real. A meta-analysis found self-report effects only, with no reliable physiological effects. | strong | `carney2016`, `forrt-powerposing` | interviewing.md |
| 18 | "A firm handshake matters." | **True-ish, small** | In a mock-interview study with 98 students, handshake quality was associated with hireability ratings, via extraversion. | moderate | `stewart2008` | interviewing.md |
| 19 | "If you're nervous, you've blown it." | **Overstated** | Anxiety correlates −.19 with interview ratings across 30 studies. That is real, but it explains a small share of outcomes. | strong | `powell2018` | interviewing.md |
| 20 | "Technical interviews objectively measure coding skill." | **Overstated** | Being watched halved performance in an RCT. One platform reports only ~20% of candidates score consistently across interviews. | moderate (RCT), weak (platform) | `behroozi2020`, `interviewingio2016` | interviewing.md |
| 21 | "Culture fit means shared values." | **Half-true** | In elite firms it often meant shared leisure pursuits and self-presentation, and could outweigh productivity. | strong (qualitative) | `rivera2012` | interviewing.md |
| 22 | "Never name a number first in a negotiation." | **Overstated** | In lab negotiations, first offers anchor in the maker's favour, unless the other side focuses on their own target and alternatives. With good market data, opening can help. | strong (lab) | `galinsky2001` | offers-negotiation.md |
| 23 | "Women don't negotiate." | **Overstated** | When ads state pay is negotiable, men and women negotiate at the same rate (21.4% vs 21.2%). The gap appears under ambiguity. Women also face backlash for asking. | strong | `leibbrandt2015`, `bowles2007` | offers-negotiation.md |
| 24 | "Negotiating is always free; the worst they can say is no." | **False for some candidates** | Evaluators penalised women more for initiating negotiations, and more-biased evaluators penalised Black negotiators with lower salaries. | strong | `bowles2007`, `hernandez2019` | offers-negotiation.md |
| 25 | "Pay transparency raises everyone's pay." | **Unsupported / likely false on average** | Laws protecting pay discussion reduced average wages by about 2% by weakening individual bargaining, while compressing gaps. | strong | `cullen2023` | offers-negotiation.md |
| 26 | "80% of people who accept a counteroffer leave within 6 months." | **Unsupported** | No traceable study; the figure varies from 48% to 93%. A CEB ~50%-in-12-months figure exists secondhand, unverified. Mostly spread by recruiters. | myth-or-unsupported | `interviewguys-counteroffer`, `4dayweek-counteroffer` | offers-negotiation.md |
| 27 | "Job hoppers always earn more." | **Overstated** | Switchers' median wage growth beat stayers' in 91% of months since 1997, by only 0.7 points on average. It peaked at 2.2 points in 2022, vanished in 2025, and reversed around 2009–10. Many short stints also cut callbacks by 40–50%. | strong | `atlantafed-wgt`, `cohn2021` | switching.md |
| 28 | "Any gap on your resume is fatal." | **Overstated** | *Current* unemployment lowers callbacks, steeply over the first 8 months. *Past* unemployment followed by work showed no effect in a Swedish experiment. Some employers do filter on gaps over six months. | strong | `kroft2013`, `eriksson2014`, `hiddenworkers2021` | switching.md |
| 29 | "Ban-the-box laws help all applicants with records." | **Overstated / mixed** | Removing the question widened the white–Black callback gap from about 7% to 43–45% among affected employers (statistical discrimination). | strong | `agan2018` | offers-negotiation.md |
| 30 | "Non-competes are unenforceable everywhere now (the FTC banned them)." | **False** | The FTC rule was vacated and the appeal dismissed in Sept 2025. State law governs in the US: California voids them, others enforce reasonable ones. They are enforceable in the UK if reasonable. In India, post-employment non-competes are generally void. | strong | `uschamber-ryan`, `loeb2023-california`, `govuk-notice`, `barandbench-s27` | switching.md |
| 31 | "Apply in the first 24–48 hours or don't bother." | **Overstated** | One unpublished dataset from a startup found ~50% of hires had applied in the first week. Applications drop after the first few days. No causal evidence that exact timing decides outcomes. | weak | `startwire2011`, `fradkin-bu2025` | channels.md |
| 32 | "Recruitment agencies can charge you to find you a job." | **False in Great Britain** | GB agencies cannot charge work-seekers a fee for finding work, except in entertainment and modelling. | strong | `govuk-agency-fees` | channels.md |
| 33 | "Interviewers can legally ask you anything in the US." | **Half-true** | Federal law explicitly prohibits only pre-offer disability inquiries. Other protected-trait questions are not banned outright but can be evidence of discrimination. In the UK, pre-offer health questions are generally unlawful. | strong | `eeoc-prohibited`, `ukparl2020-health` | interviewing.md |

## Claims considered and left out (could not verify)
- "Men negotiate 4× more often / 57% vs 7% of graduates" (*Women Don't Ask*, 2003): the sample and method could not be verified from primary material in this pass. **Do not print.**
- "Thank-you notes raise your offer odds by X%": only vendor surveys were found. Not opened, not used.
- "STAR was invented by DDI in 1974": attribution unverified. Say "a widely taught answer structure".
